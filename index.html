NRG-Twins

Pixel-Level Ground Truth for Rigidity Violations in Generated Videos

Chenrui Fan · Qiyang Hu · Luca Scharr · Paolo Favaro University of Bern
Paper arXiv soon Dataset Code BibTeX

Abstract

Video generators now produce realistic clips of scenes and objects, which makes generated video an attractive source of 3D content. Yet generated video is not 3D consistent: static content slowly deforms or slides, 3D reconstruction degrades, and the errors are too subtle to label by hand. To make generators 3D consistent, or to build rewards and guidance that push them there, we need data that say where and by how much a generated video breaks rigidity, and these data must come from generated video itself.

We obtain per-pixel ground-truth labels from real–generated twins: a real clip paired with its re-creation by a keyframe-conditioned video generator. After inserting real keyframes so that the twin camera roughly follows the real one, we measure per pixel, in degrees, how far the twin departs from a rigid scene seen along its own camera path. We release Non-Rigidly Generated Twins (NRG-Twins), 871 twins, the code that builds them for new generators, and an official test split for benchmarking.

A detector trained on these data, the Non-Rigidly Generated Pixel Detector (NRG-PD), predicts the per-pixel angle from RGB alone. It separates inconsistent from consistent pixels with AUROC 0.85, against 0.40–0.74 for seven existing consistency and motion-segmentation methods, and its predictions improve 3DGS of video that contains generated content.

How a label is measured

A generated video has no real scene behind it, so the measurement starts from a real one. A keyframe-conditioned generator re-creates a real clip from a few of its frames. The real clip, with its cameras and depth, then serves as the reference for where content should appear if the scene were rigid.

  1. Pull the twin camera onto the real camera

    Wan generates the clip segment by segment, each conditioned on a start and end keyframe, starting from frames 0 and 80. After each round VGGT estimates the rotation gap per frame. While the largest gap exceeds 0.8°, the real frame at that gap becomes a new keyframe, up to six times. On 40 training twins the median gap falls from 1.20° to 0.30°, while the content still deforms.

  2. Project a real frame into the twin camera

    Cameras come from VGGT and COLMAP, each with bundle adjustment. Depth is the per-pixel median of seven estimates, each scaled to the sparse 3D points. The nearest real frame is lifted to 3D and projected into the twin camera: this is where a rigid scene would put each pixel.

  3. Find where the content actually is

    DIS, SEA-RAFT and RAFT estimate flow from the projection to the twin frame. A pixel keeps a residual only where at least two methods pass the forward–backward check and agree.

  4. Measure the angle and subtract the noise

    The NRG angle θ is the angle between the two viewing rays, so it does not depend on image resolution. Keyframes, whose true angle is zero, give the per-frame noise σ̂, and the released label is θc = √max(min(θ, 3°)² − σ̂², 0).

The dataset

Twins are split by whole YouTube video at 8:1:1, so no source video is shared between training and the other splits. After keyframe exclusion and noise subtraction, about 65% of the labelled pixels have angles below 0.075° and about 2.3% are NRG pixels.

People cannot label it by eye

Seven raters saw the same real clip twice, one copy with a known rigid 2D deformation of one region, and were asked which side was deformed. At angles from 0.15° to 2.4°, only 2 to 16 of 42 judgements per angle picked the deformed side.

NRG-PD: the angle from RGB alone

NRG-PD reads a 24-frame generated clip and predicts the NRG angle of every pixel, without cameras, depth, optical flow or a reference video.

It keeps the 24 attention layer pairs of VGGT-1B frozen and adds LoRA branches of rank 16 to the last 8 pairs. A DPT head, initialised from VGGT’s depth head, outputs one value per pixel and frame (36.85 M trainable parameters). The target is the noise-subtracted squared angle in units of 0.6°, so the model needs no noise estimate at inference.

Prompt-generated video without labels

We generated 192 clips of 81 frames from 48 static-scene prompts (8 scenes × 3 camera motions × 2 motion scales) with LTX-2.5, CogVideoX1.5-5B, Wan2.2-T2V-A14B and HunyuanVideo-1.5. Per clip, each method labels its top and bottom 30% of point tracks as inconsistent and clean. Cameras are estimated from the clean tracks, and every remaining track is scored by its time-normalised 90th-percentile reprojection error. Track AUROC asks whether the tracks a method flags have the larger error.

Better 3D reconstruction of generated content

All fits use RobustSplat with the same settings; masked pixels or dropped frames are left out of the loss, and every rule is compared with an equal-size random mask and with no mask.

Deformed regions. One region of a real clip is warped in 2D in frames 28–51 (17 clips). Masking each pixel whose predicted angle exceeds its own median over the clip by 0.06° beats a random mask by +3.07 dB inside the warped region, better on 82% of clips, and recovers about a third of the gain of the ground-truth mask (+8.54 dB). Because this deformation is artificial, these masks come from NRG-PD trained also on injected clips.

Generated frames inside a real clip. For 11 test clips × 2 seeds, RealEstate10K frames 25–55 are replaced by Wan frames generated only from frames 24 and 56, and the same real clip is appended in reverse so that every generated view also appears as a real frame. A frame is dropped when its mean predicted angle stands out from the 6 frames with the nearest cameras. Dropping these frames beats random dropping by +4.36 dB and no masking by +3.40 dB, and closes a per-pair median of 0.70 of the gap between no masking and a fit on the all-real video.

Download and use

Each {train,val,test}/<source_id>/ folder holds real.mp4, twin.mp4, cameras.npz, labels.npz and depth.npz. An extended tier, full_truth/ (70,551 per-frame archives, about 972 GB), adds the intermediates behind every label: consensus flow, the projected real reference, rigid projections, masks and reason codes. The base tier is enough to train and evaluate detectors.

Base tier only
huggingface-cli download cvg-unibe/NRG-Twins --repo-type dataset \
